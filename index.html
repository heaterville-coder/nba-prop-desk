Your nightly routine

  • Tonight's games are already loaded. Tap a game to see both teams sorted by minutes, with shot attempts next to them.
  • If you have the game total and spread from the book, type them at the top of the game. Every projection in that game uses them.
  • Tap a player, pick the stat, and type the line and both odds from BetMGM or William Hill. The projection and edge show up as you type. Tap Add prop to keep it.
  • On My props, everything you added is ranked by edge. Tap Log paper trade on picks you'd bet, then enter the real number after the game.

Ladders and target prices

  • When you tap a player, a ladder shows his chance of reaching each number. "19+" means 19 or more.
  • Fair price is what the odds would be with no house edge. Strong at is the worst price you should accept for a Strong bet. If the book offers that price or better (for example -150 when Strong at says -200), it's a Strong bet.
  • Type the book's price next to any rung to see the verdict, then tap Add to save it to My props.
  • For a regular over/under, use the Line, Over and Under boxes at the top instead. Leave Under blank for over-only bets.

Injuries

  • Trends in the player panel: his last 10 games against the line (green over, red under), splits for home and away, back-to-backs, last 5 and vs tonight's opponent, plus written notes on anything that stands out. The row that matches tonight is highlighted. You can pull last season's games vs this opponent for reference; they never feed the projection.
  • Gold coin players are Coin Respectors: the players your crew trusts day in and day out, with their trusted markets (Pts, Reb, Ast, 3s). Their trusted stats have a ★ in the Stat menu. Tap one in the Coin Respectors row to jump straight to his prop. The Model check tracks Coin Respector picks separately, so you can see if they really are more reliable.
  • Each game lists who's out or questionable. Out players are greyed and moved to the bottom of the roster.
  • If a player isn't on the book's prop board, he's probably not playing. Tap him and hit Mark out. One teammate steps into his spot and gets up to 12 of his minutes; the desk picks the bench player at the same position, and you can change it under Who takes his spot? The rest of his minutes are spread thin across the team. Players getting extra minutes show a green +min tag. Tap Undo out to reverse it.
  • For a bigger role change than that, type the minutes into the player's Minutes box.
  • On the live version, the injury list updates on its own every 2 minutes while the page is open, and again whenever you come back to it.
  • Season stats and the schedule (with each game's spread, total and tip time) also update on their own on the live version, about every 30 minutes. If the live stats ever look incomplete, the page keeps the saved ones instead.
  • When a player is newly ruled Out, the desk treats him like you marked him out: his replacement and teammates get his minutes, and their projections update automatically. He gets a purple New tag.
  • Alerts at the top tell you when a player on one of your saved props is ruled out or questionable, or when a teammate's absence changes his rating.

Reading the roster table

  • MIN and FGA are per game this season. More minutes and more shots mean more chances to hit an over.
  • S marks a player who has started most of his games.
  • A grey row means he isn't in the newest stat pull, so his numbers are from an earlier pull this season.

How the projection is built

The desk uses this season's games only, with nothing from last year. It takes each player's per-minute production this season and multiplies it by his expected minutes. Players with fewer than 5 games get a wider range, because a few games can swing a lot. A high game total scales counting stats up, and a big spread trims a starter's minutes for blowout risk.

How the edge is figured

The projection becomes a probability of going over or under, using a typical game-to-game swing for that stat. That is compared to the payout from your odds. EV is the expected profit per $1 bet. Strong is 7% to 20%, Lean is 3% to 7%, and Pass is below 3%. Anything 20% or more is marked Check first: real edges are rarely that big, so it usually means the model is missing news, a team change, or blowout risk.

What it doesn't know

Injuries, rest, and lineup news. Check those first, and use the minutes box when a role changes. Deep bench players outside each team's main rotation aren't loaded yet.
